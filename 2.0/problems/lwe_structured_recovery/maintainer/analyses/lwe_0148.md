## Interaction and limits of the claim

The normal-LWE run substitutes uniform q-ary `A` for this sparse q-ary matrix. It is a heuristic proxy, not an upper-bound reduction and not evidence of hardness. Sparse evaluation touches only 3,174 active coefficients, signed cancellation can strengthen subset-sum/MITM variants, and the 65-residue acceptance window creates substantial collisions. On this seed, however, no two supports coincide, so the simplest identical-support cancellation has no input; the raw row-support domain has `C(130,23)=2^84.076` possibilities.

The row weight satisfies `k=23=ceil(2*sqrt(130))`, with `k^2/n=529/130=4.0692`; consequently BBTV's finite retained-sample factor `1-3/l-k^2/n` is negative for every positive `l`. This record is also outside the `k=o(sqrt(n))` premise of the [sparse-LWE lower-bound result](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12512), and its planted balanced exact-weight signed secret is not that theorem's uniform q-ary target. Because both matrix and secret are structured, no noninteraction is assumed and BBTV is not instantiated; this is not a hardness claim. The planted secret entropy is 72.668 bits; the verifier-language domain is 74.923 bits. Both are explicitly counted, so the [entropy-LWE lower bound](https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/119) is not used. The largest uncertainties are the uniform-to-sparse matrix substitution, balanced-sign interactions, and conversion of estimator rop to wall time.
