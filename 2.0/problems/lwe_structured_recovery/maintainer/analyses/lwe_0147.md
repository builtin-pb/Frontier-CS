## Interaction and limits of the claim

The estimator assumes uniform q-ary `A`, whereas this public matrix is sparse. It is a heuristic normal-LWE proxy for comparison, **not** a reduction-backed upper bound for this nonuniform instance and not a hardness claim. Sparse rows lower arithmetic cost and may enable attacks absent from the proxy. Conversely, the realized support graph has no repeated supports to feed a direct row-differencing attack; exhaustively choosing one row support already spans `C(130,23) = 2^84.076` sets. Neither observation rules out better graph, syndrome, or lattice/structure hybrids.

Here `k=23=ceil(2*sqrt(130))`, so `k^2/n=529/130=4.0692`. Thus the finite retained-sample factor `1-3/l-k^2/n` in the [BBTV sparse-LWE lower-bound result](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12512) is negative for every positive `l`, and this matrix also misses the theorem's asymptotic `k=o(sqrt(n))` premise. The planted exact-weight binary secret is not BBTV's uniform q-ary target, so the theorem is not instantiated; this is a scope check, not a hardness claim. The binary secret entropy is exactly `log2 C(130,14)=60.923` bits and is explicitly exposed to enumeration. No [entropy-LWE lower bound](https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/119) is used to transfer hardness. Runtime normalization, sparse-matrix interaction, and residual-pruning throughput are the instance-specific uncertainties.
