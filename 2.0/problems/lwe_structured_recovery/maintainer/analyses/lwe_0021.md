## Analytical model and placement

The literature route is the full-guess specialization of
[Son--Cheon 2019, Section 3.1, Algorithm 1](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/1019.pdf):
guessing the whole sparse secret removes the residual lattice stage and leaves
enumeration of the exact public support/value domain followed by witness
validation.  The checked-in `sparse_secret_enum` is an independent clean-room
implementation of only that degenerate component.  Because it traverses every
secret in the public predicate, the narrow mapping is independent of `A` and
the error law and is paper-case eligible here; it transfers no lattice
preprocessing, Gaussian success analysis, paper constant, or claim of fidelity
to the complete Algorithm 1.

`C(114,2) * 2^2 = 25,764 = 2^14.653069` candidates.

## Interaction and alternative attacks

Enumeration is a complete upper bound because it covers every verifier-valid
secret independently of the matrix distribution. Its runtime constant still
depends on dense column accumulation, 236-row validation, rejection depth, and
the 193-value residual window; the recorded `1/n` rate law does not separately
model those effects or `m`.

A 57+57 split has 6,499 signed partial assignments of weights at most two on
each side. Nonzero noise invalidates an ordinary exact-key MITM join, while
four approximate coordinates already admit `193^4=1,387,488,001` error
offsets. A better filtered MITM could still win, but its collision and memory
costs are unpriced. The Gaussian table's zero mass is about 0.0124990, making
an all-clean 114-row subsystem only about `2^-720.713`; clean-subset solving is
not the selected route. Primal BDD and lattice hybrids remain unmeasured
alternatives. Full-secret enumeration, `769^114 = 2^1092.900`, is merely a
loose upper bound, not a hardness floor or Lattice Estimator result.
