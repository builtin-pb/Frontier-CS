`p=C(359,123)/C(369,123)=0.0162938481598`.

The frozen v2 calculation is

`r=max(80*(16/123)^1.5,2)=3.7532915` trials/second. It predicts
`W_50/r=11.3341643` seconds, within E1's `[4,16)` interval; the independent
slot target is 11.3137085 seconds. The exact mean clean-support waiting time is
`1/p=61.3728562`, so the recorded `ln(2)/p` is explicitly a continuous median

Rank 123 establishes at least one usable square minor but does not make every
clean 123-subset nonsingular. The model leaves singular-minor frequency,
ternary elimination constants, and validation cost inside its extrapolated
rate rather than measuring them on this instance.

This is the clean-subset route discussed in
[Sun--Tibouchi--Abe, IACR ePrint 2020/666, §4.2](https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/666):
seek an error-free square subsystem, solve it, and validate the resulting
candidate against the full record. The local solver is a clean-room generalized
adaptation of that attack shape, not a source-identical implementation, and it
does not inherit the paper's asymptotics, constants, or success assumptions.
Here the hypergeometric
support-avoidance probability is exact for the planted fixed-weight support;
minor invertibility and implementation throughput remain separate caveats.
