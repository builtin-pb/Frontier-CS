## Interaction and limits of the claim

The estimator calculation assumes uniform q-ary rows, whereas every public row has weight 20. It is a heuristic normal-LWE comparator only: it is neither a reduction-backed upper bound for the sparse matrix nor a hardness claim. Sparse evaluation and secret guessing can interact through low-degree columns, although this realization's degree minimum is 13 rather than an obvious isolated coordinate. No complete supports repeat. The default support-selection space is `C(100,20)=2^68.861`, but partial graph attacks need not traverse it.

Here `k=20=ceil(2*sqrt(100))`, so `k^2/n=400/100=4.0000` and BBTV's finite retained-sample factor `1-3/l-k^2/n` is negative for every positive `l`. The `k=o(sqrt(n))` premise of the [sparse-LWE lower-bound result](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12512) is deliberately not met, and the planted exact-weight binary secret differs from its uniform q-ary target. BBTV is therefore not instantiated; the simultaneous structures prevent a matrix-only reading, and this is not a hardness claim. Secret entropy is only 55.294 bits and is already represented by the explicit direct enumeration; no [entropy-LWE lower bound](https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/119) supports the placement. The H3 label is an extrapolated portfolio estimate whose weak points are sparse-matrix interaction and rop calibration.
