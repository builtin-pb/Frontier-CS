## Analytical model and placement

The literature route is the full-guess specialization of
[Son--Cheon 2019, Section 3.1, Algorithm 1](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/1019.pdf):
guessing the whole sparse secret removes the residual lattice stage and leaves
enumeration of the exact public support/value domain followed by witness
validation.  The checked-in `sparse_secret_enum` is an independent clean-room
implementation of only that degenerate component.  Because it traverses every
secret in the public predicate, the narrow mapping is independent of `A` and
the error law and is paper-case eligible here; it transfers no lattice
preprocessing, Gaussian success analysis, paper constant, or claim of fidelity
to the complete Algorithm 1.

For `analytical-secret-enumeration-v1`, the exact work is

`C(42,7) * 1^7 = 26,978,328 = 2^24.685298` candidates.

The spec's formula is
`binomial(n,h)*nonzero_alphabet_size^h/(2.4e+06/n)`.  It binds
`nonzero_alphabet_size=1`, `measured_candidate_rate_n_product=1,200,000`, and
rate model `2*measured_candidate_rate_n_product/n`.  The assumed throughput is
`2,400,000/42 = 57,142.857143` candidates/second, yielding
`26,978,328/57,142.857143 = 472.120740000` seconds.  That prediction lies in
`E4=[256,1024)` and is close to the 477.712891667-second design target.  The
throughput product is a cross-instance model input, not a current-record
timing measurement.

## Interaction and alternative attacks

Direct enumeration is complete for the public secret predicate and its domain
size does not depend on `A`.  Runtime does depend on the dense 92-by-42
validation, streaming rejection and caching, and the 1,023-value residual
window (roughly one quarter of `q`).  These constants and `m` are not
separately represented by the recorded `1/n` rate law.

A symmetric 21+21 split has 198,440 partial supports per side when weights up
to seven are retained.  Exact-key matching is invalid in the presence of
nonzero error, and four approximate matching coordinates allow
`1023^4=1,095,222,947,841` offsets.  MITM remains unpriced and a sophisticated
filter could outperform enumeration, but a naive join has substantial memory,
collision, and noise-neighborhood costs.  The planted finite Gaussian table
has zero mass about 0.00234841, so a clean 42-row subsystem occurs with
probability about `2^-366.832`; clean-subset solving is not competitive.
Primal BDD or lattice hybrids remain unmeasured alternatives.  Full-secret
enumeration would be `4093^42 = 2^503.956` trials, a very loose upper bound and
not a hardness floor or estimator calibration.
