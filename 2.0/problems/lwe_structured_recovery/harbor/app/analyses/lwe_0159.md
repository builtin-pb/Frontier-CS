## Interaction and limits of the claim

This normal-LWE calculation assumes uniform q-ary `A`; it is a heuristic proxy for the row-weight-25 matrix, not an upper-bound reduction and not evidence of hardness. Sparse arithmetic may reduce hybrid costs, while the absence of repeated supports only excludes a simple whole-row cancellation. Exhaustive row-support selection has `C(150,25)=2^93.982` possibilities, but partial graph and syndrome attacks are not bounded by that number.

The matrix setting `k=25=ceil(2*sqrt(150))` gives `k^2/n=625/150=4.1667`, so BBTV's finite retained-sample factor `1-3/l-k^2/n` is negative for every positive `l`. It is also outside the `k=o(sqrt(n))` regime of the [sparse-LWE lower-bound result](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12512), and the planted exact-weight binary secret is not its uniform q-ary target. BBTV is not instantiated; no matrix-only theorem, noninteraction assumption, or hardness claim is used. The secret domain has 63.957 bits of entropy and is openly counted by enumeration; no [entropy-LWE](https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/119) hardness transfer underwrites H9. The estimator-to-time normalization and possible sparse-matrix/hybrid crossover remain the dominant uncertainty.
