## Analytical model and placement

The literature route is the full-guess specialization of
[Son--Cheon 2019, Section 3.1, Algorithm 1](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/1019.pdf):
guessing the whole sparse secret removes the residual lattice stage and leaves
enumeration of the exact public support/value domain followed by witness
validation.  The checked-in `sparse_secret_enum` is an independent clean-room
implementation of only that degenerate component.  Because it traverses every
secret in the public predicate, the narrow mapping is independent of `A` and
the error law and is paper-case eligible here; it transfers no lattice
preprocessing, Gaussian success analysis, paper constant, or claim of fidelity
to the complete Algorithm 1.

The selected `analytical-secret-enumeration-v1` domain is

`C(45,5) * 1^5 = 1,221,759 = 2^20.220528` candidates.

The checked-in formula is
`binomial(n,h)*nonzero_alphabet_size^h/(2.4e+06/n)`, with
`nonzero_alphabet_size=1`, `measured_candidate_rate_n_product=1,200,000`, and
rate law `2*measured_candidate_rate_n_product/n`.  The resulting assumed rate
is `2,400,000/45 = 53,333.333333` candidates/second, so the prediction is
`1,221,759/53,333.333333 = 22.907981250` seconds.  This falls in
`E2=[16,64)` and is close to the 22.627416998-second design target.  The
throughput product is a parameter of the cross-instance model, not a measured
runtime of this instance.

## Interaction and alternative attacks

The enumeration count covers the complete accepted secret language and is
matrix-distribution-independent.  Its wall-clock constant is not: dense
column summation, the 98-row validation path, early rejection, and a
307-value residual window all interact.  The spec's `1/n` scaling does not
model `m` or the realized rejection depth, so the runtime remains a
hypothesis.

A 22+23 split contains 35,443 and 44,552 partial supports of weights at most
five.  Nonzero error invalidates a direct exact-key join, while four
approximate residual coordinates admit `307^4=8,882,874,001` error offsets.
MITM is unpriced and a stronger filter could beat enumeration, but a naive join
faces substantial collision, memory, and filtering costs.
The exact finite Gaussian table has zero mass about 0.00784290 per coordinate;
an all-clean 45-row subsystem is only about `2^-314.748`, so clean-subset
solving is not a credible primary route.  Primal BDD and lattice hybrids
remain unmeasured alternatives.  Full mod-`q` enumeration would require
`1229^45 = 2^461.847` trials and is only a loose universal upper bound, not a
lattice estimate.

## Calibration and lower-bound scope

For these exact release bytes, the first `sparse_secret_enum` probe was
`CENSORED` at the 16-second lower-edge cap. The subsequent full E2 replay in
[`CALIBRATION.md`](../CALIBRATION.md) recovered the witness at seed 0 under a
90-second cap, and the production evaluator accepted it. The concurrent replay
retained no portable per-instance wall time, so the public spec remains
`calibration_status=extrapolated` with `measured_runtime_seconds=null`.

Secret min-entropy is exactly 20.220528 bits and the direct attack explicitly
counts that domain.  Although the named planted error law is a truncated
discrete Gaussian, no entropy-LWE conclusion follows without an instantiated
finite-parameter map and every Brakerski--Döttling precondition; none is
claimed here.  Dense uniform `A` is outside the matrix-sparse scope of the
cited sparse-LWE lower bound.  These fail-closed scope statements are not
proofs against other attacks, and `E2` is not a hardness claim.
