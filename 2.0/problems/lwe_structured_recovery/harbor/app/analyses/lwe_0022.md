## Analytical model and placement

The literature route is the full-guess specialization of
[Son--Cheon 2019, Section 3.1, Algorithm 1](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/1019.pdf):
guessing the whole sparse secret removes the residual lattice stage and leaves
enumeration of the exact public support/value domain followed by witness
validation.  The checked-in `sparse_secret_enum` is an independent clean-room
implementation of only that degenerate component.  Because it traverses every
secret in the public predicate, the narrow mapping is independent of `A` and
the error law and is paper-case eligible here; it transfers no lattice
preprocessing, Gaussian success analysis, paper constant, or claim of fidelity
to the complete Algorithm 1.

## Interaction and alternative attacks

The exact enumeration domain is a complete, matrix-independent attack upper
bound. Actual throughput still depends on the dense 50-by-21 validation,
streaming rejection, caching, and the 307-value residual window; the `1/n`
rate scaling does not fully price those interactions.

A 10+11 coordinate split gives 12,585 and 21,627 signed partial assignments
of weights at most five. Nonzero error prevents exact-key joining, and four
approximate coordinates allow `307^4=8,882,874,001` offsets. MITM could beat
enumeration with a better multi-stage filter, but no such crossover is
measured. Since one bounded-uniform error is zero with probability `1/307`, an
all-clean 21-row subsystem has probability `307^-21 = 2^-173.504`, so
clean-subset recovery is not primary. Primal BDD and lattice hybrids are
unmeasured alternatives. The fallback `1229^21 = 2^215.529` full-secret trials
is a loose upper bound, not a lower bound or estimator calibration.
