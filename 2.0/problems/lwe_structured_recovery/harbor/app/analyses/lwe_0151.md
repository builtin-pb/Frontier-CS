## Interaction and limits of the claim

The estimator's matrix is uniform q-ary, not this row-weight-19 public matrix. Its result is a heuristic comparator, not a reduction-backed upper bound for the structured instance and not a hardness claim. Here uSVP is only 0.330 bits above hybrid, so sparse linear algebra or implementation constants could change the fastest practical method. The 65/257 centered-residue acceptance window also makes partial-sum buckets collision-heavy.

No row supports repeat on this seed, withholding the simplest support-cancellation shortcut; enumerating all row supports would cost `C(85,19)=2^61.884` choices. This is not a lower bound: graph elimination and mixed lattice/guessing attacks can exploit sparse `A` without repeating a whole support.

The row weight is `k=19=ceil(2*sqrt(85))`, and `k^2/n=361/85=4.2471` makes BBTV's finite retained-sample factor `1-3/l-k^2/n` negative for every positive `l`. It is also outside the `k=o(sqrt(n))` scope of the [sparse-LWE lower-bound result](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12512), and the planted exact-weight binary secret is not the theorem's uniform q-ary target. Matrix and secret are both structured, so BBTV is not instantiated and no noninteraction or hardness theorem is assumed. The exact secret entropy is 51.755 bits and the direct attack counts it explicitly; no [entropy-LWE lower bound](https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/119) is used. H1 remains extrapolated through estimator normalization, with sparse-matrix effects as the main instance-specific uncertainty.
