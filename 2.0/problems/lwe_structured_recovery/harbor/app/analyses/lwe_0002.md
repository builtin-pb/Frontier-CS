## Analytical model and placement

The literature route is the full-guess specialization of
[Son--Cheon 2019, Section 3.1, Algorithm 1](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/1019.pdf):
guessing the whole sparse secret removes the residual lattice stage and leaves
enumeration of the exact public support/value domain followed by witness
validation.  The checked-in `sparse_secret_enum` is an independent clean-room
implementation of only that degenerate component.  Because it traverses every
secret in the public predicate, the narrow mapping is independent of `A` and
the error law and is paper-case eligible here; it transfers no lattice
preprocessing, Gaussian success analysis, paper constant, or claim of fidelity
to the complete Algorithm 1.

The primary `analytical-secret-enumeration-v1` work factor is

`C(25,7) * 1^7 = 480,700 = 2^18.874777` candidates.

The exact spec formula is
`binomial(n,h)*nonzero_alphabet_size^h/(2.4e+06/n)`.  It records
`nonzero_alphabet_size=1`, `measured_candidate_rate_n_product=1,200,000`, and
`rate_model=2*measured_candidate_rate_n_product/n`.  Substitution gives an
assumed rate of `2,400,000/25 = 96,000` candidates/second and
`480,700/96,000 = 5.007291667` predicted seconds.  The prediction is in
`E1=[4,16)` and is close to the independent 4.924577653-second design target.
The recorded rate product is a calibration input to the analytical model, not
`lwe_0002` wall-clock metadata.

## Interaction and alternative attacks

Exact enumeration remains a complete attack upper bound for every accepted
secret and its candidate count is independent of `A`.  Actual evaluation cost
is not: dense column accumulation, `m=58`, streaming rejection depth, and the
193-value residual window determine the constant.  The recorded `1/n` rate
law does not resolve those interactions or guarantee the predicted wall time.

A 12+13 split produces 3,302 and 5,812 partial subsets of weights at most
seven.  Nonzero error prevents a direct exact-key join, and a four-row
approximate signature has `193^4=1,387,488,001` allowed error offsets.  MITM is
unpriced and may still beat enumeration with stronger filtering, but a naive
join has a very large neighborhood and memory/collision cost.  Bounded-uniform error is
zero with probability `1/193`; an all-clean 25-row subsystem occurs with
probability `193^-25 = 2^-189.811`, making clean-subset recovery implausible.
Primal BDD and lattice-hybrid attacks are separate unmeasured possibilities.
The universal full-secret fallback is `769^25 = 2^239.671` candidates, but
that count is not an estimator calibration or a lower bound.
